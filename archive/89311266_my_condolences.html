My Sincere Condolences

When Agatha Harkness was forced to marry her cousin, Todd Harkness, at the age of twenty, she believed it would be the worst day of her life.

She was well beyond the age society considered ideal for an omega to marry, yet still far too young for a fifty-two-year-old alpha who had already been widowed twice. His two previous wives had died of influenza, and everyone said Todd deserved a third chance.

No one asked what Agatha wanted.

The sudden death of her father, Mephisto Harkness, left Evanora determined to preserve the family name, and the marriage was treated as a duty rather than a choice.

Agatha learned early on that, for women like her, there was very little difference between a family decision and a sentence.

The honeymoon only confirmed what she already knew.

Intimacy had never felt natural to her. She endured that night in silence, feeling completely detached from her own body. Afterward, she remained in the bath for a long time, letting the water run over her skin as she tried to wash away the emptiness that clung to her.

Above all, she was grateful she had not become pregnant during those few months of marriage.

It was not only because she did not want children at that moment.

She had simply never felt any attraction toward male alphas.

Eight months later, Todd died of severe pneumonia, and just as quickly as she had become a wife, Agatha became a widow.

His will caused a scandal. Todd’s entire fortune, his estates, and all of his investments were left exclusively to her.

Her brother, Mephisto Joseph Harkness Jr., immediately contested the inheritance. He argued that the family estate should remain under alpha administration, but the document was incontestable.

Legally, everything belonged to Agatha.

In practice, however, Mephisto insisted on managing his sister’s affairs, convincing Evanora that an omega should not deal directly with financial matters.

Agatha allowed it, not because she agreed, but because, by then, she was already tired of fighting battles she was never allowed to win.

Even so, Mephisto found countless ways to make her life miserable, though hit sister was hardly one to let him get away with it.

The years passed, and there was one phrase Agatha heard so many times that she lost count.

“My sincere condolences.”

Condolences for her widowhood, condolences for her loneliness, condolences for never having truly lived. As time went by, she could barely remember Todd’s face anymore.

But the condolences remained, when she turned forty, she felt a strange sense of peace.

Society practically regarded her as a relic. An omega her age would normally already have a large family, grown children running through the gardens, and grandchildren on the way.

Agatha had none of that, and for the first time, she realized she no longer had to pretend she was waiting for it.

The following morning, she opened her wardrobe. The black mourning dresses were still impeccably arranged.

She ran her fingers over the fabric for a few seconds before closing the doors. She was tired of living like a ghost.

Without completely abandoning the discretion expected of a widow, she allowed other dark colors to return to her wardrobe and reopened the doors of Harkness Manor to society.

Lady Harkness’s annual balls soon became a tradition in Westview.

It was there that the region’s young alphas and omegas were formally introduced to high society beneath crystal chandeliers, live music, and countless rules of etiquette.

Agatha observed everything from a distance, almost always with a glass of wine in her hand and a sarcastic reply ready for any inconvenient conversation.

It was during one of those balls that her routine changed once again.

In the middle of the celebration, thieves who had infiltrated the guests took advantage of the commotion to steal several valuable pieces from the manor.

By the time the last guest left the estate, the entire house was in an uproar. Mephisto spent hours accusing the servants of incompetence.

Agatha challenged every word he said, and the argument stretched on until dawn.

“This never would have happened if you managed this house with less sentimentality,” Mephisto shot back.

“Funny. The house is mine, yet you keep talking as if you own it.”

He ignored the provocation.

“Security will be reinforced.”

Melina Vostokoff, who had served as head of the estate’s security for many years, remained motionless throughout the entire argument.

“I agree,” she said firmly.

“We need to increase our personnel.”
Agatha let out a long sigh, reinforced security.

In practice, that meant more guards in the hallways, more escorts, more eyes following her every step, another layer added to the invisible bars of the gilded cage she had lived in since she was twenty.

Morning came much sooner than Agatha would have liked. Her maid, Kate, pulled open the heavy bedroom curtains, allowing the golden sunlight to flood the room, illuminating the dark mahogany furniture and the delicate embroidery of the lace curtains.

“Good morning, milady.”

“I strongly object to having to get up today. Can I say I have dysentery?”

“Unfortunately not, my lady.”

Agatha huffed as she got to her feet. Kate merely smiled, accustomed to her mistress’s bad temper, and together they dressed her as they did every day.

First the petticoat, then the corset, laced tightly enough to remind her that breathing was only a temporary privilege.

Then came the long-sleeved black dress, adorned with discreet velvet details and small onyx beads. The high collar concealed her neck completely, while an antique cameo rested against her chest. The gloves, the brooch, the black pearl earrings…

Agatha watched it all in the mirror.

“I’m a damned trinket.”

Kate did not reply. She already knew that complaint was simply part of her mistress’s daily routine.

When Agatha descended the grand staircase of Harkness Manor, she found the main hall occupied. Mephisto was delivering a speech as though he were addressing an audience.

Melina Vostokoff stood beside him, impeccably uniformed, her hands clasped behind her back.

Natasha Romanoff chatted casually while examining a map of the estate spread across the table.

Beside her, Wanda Romanoff discreetly raised a hand in greeting. Agatha merely inclined her head. At that moment, smiling would have required unnecessary effort.

She couldn’t tell whether she was in such a foul mood because the house was now infested with guards, or because of the cabbage soup from the night before, which stubbornly insisted on reminding her of its existence with a thoroughly inelegant discomfort.

Perhaps it was both.

She settled into her usual chair at the smaller head of the table, resting an elbow on the armrest as she waited for yet another one of her brother’s long demonstrations of masculine authority to come to an end.

Mephisto gestured as though every word were a decree. Natasha seemed to listen out of nothing more than courtesy, and Melina was the first to approach.

Agatha grimaced.

“An unfortunate night.” Melina subtly arched an eyebrow.

“Tie the laces, please.” Melina kept her expression perfectly neutral. Inside, however, her thoughts echoed.

After so many years of serving the Harkness family, Melina knew Agatha better than almost anyone.

She knew Agatha was capable of managing entire estates, standing up to bankers, and putting politicians in their place with half a dozen razor-sharp remarks.

But she also knew that, whenever she felt like it, she refused to perform even the simplest task simply because someone else could do it for her.

Marble fountains cast graceful arcs of crystal-clear water that reflected the soft morning light.

Watch the garden, she tried, but only a few seconds later, her eyes drifted back to the table of their own accord.

With the natural ease of someone who believed herself completely alone, she brought her finger to her lips rather than waste the sweetness.

Rio looked away immediately. Focus on your job. She took a deep breath and fixed her attention on the front door.

It lasted exactly five seconds, before she realized it, she was looking back again.

Lady Harkness’s impeccable posture contrasted curiously with the carefree way she enjoyed her sweets.

Her mourning dress, with its high collar and dark fabric, retained all the elegance expected of a widow in high society, yet the modest neckline and the way the bodice fit drew attention without seeming to intend it.

Rio cleared her throat softly. You survived an entire war, and you’re losing to a lady having tea.

The thought made her suppress a smile( Perhaps Natasha had been right. Protecting Harkness Manor would be the easy part.

The real challenge would be spending a single day beside Lady Agatha Harkness without her noticing absolutely everything happening around her

After all, if there was one thing Rio was beginning to suspect, it was that very little escaped the widow’s watchful eye, while Agatha seemed completely oblivious to the world around her.

While much of the country was going through hard times, with news of poor harvests and entire families struggling to put food on the table, Lady Harkness was fighting her own battle against a tray of desserts.

And, by all appearances… she was winning.

“There are people starving…”

“You took too long.”

“And I wasn’t the one who had to explain to your doctor why you insist on turning a ‘cheat day’ into a banquet.”

Agatha rolled her eyes with the elegance of someone who had been doing it for decades.

“I deserve a cheat day.”

“But the doctor would certainly disagree with the quantity.”

Agatha pretended not to hear her.

She was far too busy finishing another slice of cake. Melina picked up a perfectly folded linen napkin and, in a gesture that had long since become habit, leaned down slightly.

“Allow me.”

She gently wiped away a small trace of whipped cream lingering at the corner of Agatha’s lips.

The widow did not even seem to notice, or perhaps she was simply too accustomed to that kind of care.

Rio discreetly looked away, she felt as though she were witnessing a moment that had nothing to do with her.

Melina knew this routine, she  knew Agatha, those small gestures betrayed years of familiarity.

You’re working. Look anywhere else.

“Yes, ma’am.”

She carefully picked up the porcelain teapot and poured the amber-colored liquid into the cup.

The delicate aroma of bergamot filled the room once more, she then placed the teacup on the silver tray and walked over to Agatha. Lowering herself just enough to make it easier for her to reach it, she waited.

Agatha extended her hand, the moment she took hold of the teacup’s handle, her fingers lightly brushed Rio’s.

It was a brief contact, Almost insignificant and yet Rio noticed the difference immediately.

Agatha’s hands were astonishingly soft.

Her skin felt like silk, untouched by the marks of hard labor. Nothing like Rio’s own hands, hardened by years of gripping reins, swords, and rifles.

Agatha lifted her eyes for a brief moment, their gazes met, only for a second.

Then the widow calmly took a sip of her tea, as though that fleeting touch had meant absolutely nothing.

Rio, on the other hand, returned to her place, trying to convince herself that her attention should remain focused on the security of the manor.

She was beginning to suspect, however, that Lady Agatha Harkness would prove to be the most dangerous distraction in the entire house.

Melina was seated in the armchair beside the fireplace.

Without the slightest ceremony, Agatha rested her legs across the head of security’s lap, as though it were a gesture they had repeated for so many years that it no longer seemed worthy of notice.
